Tiger Balm red vs white, and Thai balms: what the colour tells you

Red balms warm and white balms cool, and the reason is on the label. Tiger Balm and Siang Pure compared, plus which to use when.

By Dan Ler, Siam Supply Co.  ·  Published 6 October 2026

Tiger Balm red vs white, and every other red and white balm, comes down to one thing: red warms and white cools. The colour follows the ingredients: red balms add a warming oil, usually cinnamon (cassia) or a large dose of methyl salicylate, while white balms lean on menthol. Use red for stiff muscles and joints, white for headaches, blocked noses and itchy bites.

"Tiger Balm red vs white" is one of the most searched balm questions in Singapore, and the same red-and-white split runs through Thai balms too. This guide compares the labels of two makers, Tiger Balm and Siang Pure, so you can see exactly where the difference comes from rather than taking the colour on trust.

What the colour actually tells you

Balm colour is not a strength rating. A white balm is not a weaker red balm; it is a different recipe aimed at a different job.

Tiger Balm: red vs white

Tiger Balm's own guidance is refreshingly plain. Both versions share camphor, menthol, and oils of clove, cajeput and field mint. The red version adds cinnamon oil, and the proportions differ.

  • Tiger Balm Red "has a warming effect". The maker suggests it for stiff muscles and joints, before and after sport, and for easing morning stiffness. It has a strong herbal, spicy smell, and the maker warns it can stain clothing and sheets.
  • Tiger Balm White "has a cooling and relaxing effect". The maker suggests it for muscle pain, rubbed on the temples for a headache, under the nose to breathe more easily, and on insect bites.

Siang Pure: red pack vs white pack

Siang Pure's balms, from the Thai maker Bertram, show the same split with different chemistry. The balm in the red pack is labelled Yellow Balm, which can confuse people. Bertram calls it the hot formula. Its lead ingredient is methyl salicylate at 29%, the compound behind wintergreen's smell and the warming "deep heat" feel, with peppermint, camphor, clove and cinnamon.

The White Balm is the cool formula, led by menthol at 28%, with peppermint, camphor and a smaller amount of methyl salicylate. Bertram lists the hot balm for muscle and joint discomfort, and the cool balm for the same plus itchy insect bites.

So where Tiger Balm gets its heat mainly from cinnamon, Siang Pure's hot balm gets it mainly from methyl salicylate. Both end up in the same place: red for heat, white for cool.

Which one to reach for

  • Stiff neck or back, sore muscles: red. Rub it in well so the warmth builds.
  • Before exercise: red, which Tiger Balm itself suggests before and after sport.
  • Headache: white, a small amount on the temples, well away from the eyes.
  • Blocked nose: white, a little under the nose. For a nose that needs clearing throughout the day, an inhaler is easier to carry; see our guide to Thai inhalers.
  • Itchy mosquito bite: white. The cold takes the edge off the itch.

How to use a balm safely

  • Use a small amount. A pea-sized dab covers a temple or a bite.
  • Keep it away from the eyes, and wash your hands after use.
  • Never put it on broken or irritated skin.
  • Do not cover the area tightly with a bandage or put a heat pack over it.
  • Keep balms away from young children, and do not use camphor products on children under two.
  • People who are allergic to aspirin should check with a pharmacist before using balms high in methyl salicylate, which is related to aspirin.

What about Thai balms in other colours?

Thai shelves also carry green balms, jelly balms and balm sticks. Most follow the same logic as the white balms: cooling, with herbal or fruit scents. The Pastel Balm Stick, for example, puts a mild balm in a twist-up stick in mango, mixed berry and orange scents, so you apply it without touching it. It is made for people who would never carry a traditional tin.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between red and white Tiger Balm?

Both contain camphor, menthol, clove, cajeput and mint oil. Red adds cinnamon oil, which makes it warming; white is cooling. Tiger Balm suggests red for stiff muscles and joints, and white for headaches, breathing and insect bites.

Which is stronger, red or white balm?

Neither is simply stronger. They work differently: red produces heat, white produces a cooling feel. Choose by the job, not by strength.

Why is the Siang Pure red balm called Yellow Balm?

The maker, Bertram, names its hot formula the Yellow Balm and sells it in a red pack. The White Balm is its cool formula.

Does red balm stain clothes?

Tiger Balm warns that its red balm can stain clothing and sheets. Let it absorb before getting dressed or going to bed.

Which balm is best for a headache?

A white, cooling balm, rubbed gently on the temples and kept away from the eyes.
