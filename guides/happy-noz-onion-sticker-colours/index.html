Happy Noz onion stickers: every colour, explained

Purple, blue, green, yellow, orange and Bug Guard: what each Happy Noz sticker adds, where the patch goes, and how it differs from an inhaler.

By Dan Ler, Siam Supply Co.  ·  Published 13 October 2026

Happy Noz is a Thai onion-oil sticker you put on clothing, not on skin, so a child breathes the scent without anything touching the face. Every colour starts from the same onion base. The colour tells you which extra oil the maker has added: tea tree in blue, turmeric in green, ginger in yellow, clove in orange. Purple is the original, and Bug Guard is a separate mosquito sticker.

Happy Noz is searched around 320 times a month in Singapore, mostly by parents trying to work out which box to buy. It is also the line childcare centres and family pharmacies order from us in the largest volumes, and "which colour?" is the question we are asked most. Here is the full answer.

What Happy Noz is

Happy Noz is made in Thailand. The maker describes it as built on a selected red onion variety rich in quercetin, blended with essential oils. The onion scent is the traditional part. A cut onion by the bed is an old folk remedy for a blocked nose, and the sticker is a tidier way of doing the same thing.

The important difference from a Thai inhaler is where it goes. A menthol or camphor inhaler is not suitable for young children. A Happy Noz sticker sits on a collar, a pillow or the side of a cot, so the child gets the scent without handling anything or having a strong product near the face.

Purple: the original

The one to start with. It is the plain onion formula with eucalyptus, peppermint and lavender, for a stuffy or runny nose. If you only buy one box, buy this.

Blue: tea tree

Adds tea tree oil, which the maker includes for its antibacterial reputation. Some parents reach for blue when a cold has moved on and the mucus has turned thick and yellow.

Green: turmeric

Adds turmeric oil, which the maker positions against viruses. It is the colour parents tend to pick at the start of a cold going round the class.

Yellow: ginger

Adds ginger oil, which the maker includes to soothe coughs. The one for a child who is coughing at night rather than just congested.

Orange: clove, for haze

Adds clove oil, which the maker includes for PM2.5 pollution. In Singapore that makes it the seasonal one, bought when the haze comes back.

Bug Guard: a different product

Bug Guard is not an onion nasal sticker at all. It is a mosquito-repellent sticker from the same company, with a cartoon frog on the pack. Do not buy it expecting help with a blocked nose. For mosquito protection on skin, a DEET spray such as SOFFELL is the stronger option.

How to use a Happy Noz sticker

  1. Peel one sticker. One at a time is enough.
  2. Stick it on clothing, at the collar or on the chest, or on a pillowcase or the side of a cot. Not directly on skin.
  3. Place it out of reach of a baby's hands, so it cannot be pulled off and put in the mouth.
  4. Replace it with a fresh one when the scent has faded.

A sticker is small enough to be a choking hazard, so supervise young children and put it somewhere they cannot reach. Check the age guidance printed on the box, and speak to a doctor if a baby's breathing seems laboured, if there is a fever, or if symptoms last more than a few days. A sticker is a comfort, not a treatment.

Happy Noz vs a Thai inhaler

They are for different people. A Thai inhaler, the yadom, is menthol and camphor that an adult holds under the nose; it should not be used on children under two. Happy Noz is onion-based, goes on clothing, and is made with children in mind. Many families keep both: the inhaler for the adults, the sticker for the kids. For adults who prefer a sticker, the maker also sells an adult range.

Questions people ask

What do the Happy Noz colours mean?

All colours share an onion base. Purple is the original with eucalyptus, peppermint and lavender; blue adds tea tree oil; green adds turmeric oil; yellow adds ginger oil; orange adds clove oil for PM2.5. Bug Guard is a separate mosquito sticker.

Where do you put a Happy Noz sticker?

On clothing near the collar or chest, or on a pillow or cot. Not directly on the skin, and always out of a baby's reach.

Which Happy Noz is for cough?

Yellow, which adds ginger oil. The maker includes it to soothe coughs.

Is Happy Noz Bug Guard the same as Happy Noz?

No. Bug Guard is a mosquito-repellent sticker, not an onion nasal sticker.
