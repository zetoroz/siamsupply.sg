Medicated oils

Namman Muay boxing liniment: what is in it and how to use it

What the Thai boxing oil contains, how to use it before and after training, and who should check with a doctor first, from the maker's own label.

By Dan Ler, Siam Supply Co.  ·  Published 30 October 2026

Namman Muay is a Thai sports liniment, a thin yellow oil you massage into muscles and joints. Its two active ingredients are methyl salicylate and menthol. Its maker, Devakam Apothecary Hall in Thailand, labels it for the temporary relief of minor aches and pains: backache, strains, sprains, bruises and arthritis. In Thai boxing camps it is the oil trainers rub into a fighter's legs before a session.

The name is plain Thai: namman means oil and muay means boxing. The bottle is easy to spot, yellow with a white cap and footballers and boxers printed on the label, and the Thai text under the brand reads "for athletes". This guide covers what is in it, how to use it, and the few rules that matter with a salicylate rub.

What is in Namman Muay

The Thai label names the two actives, menthol and methyl salicylate, in brackets under the brand. The maker's US registration gives the strengths: methyl salicylate 31% and menthol 1.25%, in a base of alcohol, mineral oil, eucalyptus oil, purified water and an emulsifier. That emulsifier is why the label says to shake the bottle first.

  • Methyl salicylate is the compound that gives wintergreen its smell. It is a counterirritant: it warms the skin and the warmth takes your attention off the ache underneath. It is also a chemical relative of aspirin, which matters for the warnings below.
  • Menthol gives the cool tingle on top. At 1.25% it is a minor partner here, the opposite of a Thai inhaler, where menthol is usually the largest ingredient.

In other words, Namman Muay is closer to a liquid version of a red, warming balm than to a cooling oil. If you have read our guide to red and white balm, it sits firmly on the red side.

How to use it

  1. Shake the bottle. The oil and water separate when it stands.
  2. Pour a little into your palm, not straight onto the skin, so you can control how much goes on. A few drops covers a calf or a shoulder.
  3. Massage it in until it is absorbed. The maker's direction is to work it into the area, not leave it sitting on top.
  4. Wash your hands afterwards, and keep them away from your eyes until you have.

The label allows use up to four times a day for anyone aged 12 and over. For younger children it says to ask a doctor first.

Before training or after?

Both, and the two uses are different. In Thai boxing camps it goes on before training: a trainer rubs it into the shins, thighs and shoulders as part of the warm-up massage, and the heat on the skin is part of the ritual. After training, people use it the way the label describes, on a sore muscle, a knock or a stiff joint.

Two cautions apply to both. Do not use it on skin that is cut, scraped or broken, which in a contact sport is often the shin. And it is a warming rub, not a treatment for an injury: a swollen ankle or a joint that will not bear weight needs looking at, not oil.

Who should be careful

  • People allergic to aspirin or other salicylates. The maker's label tells them to ask a doctor before use, because methyl salicylate is in the same family.
  • Anyone on blood-thinning medicine, such as warfarin, should ask a pharmacist before using a salicylate rub regularly or over a large area.
  • Children under 12: ask a doctor, as the label says. Keep the bottle out of reach. Methyl salicylate is harmful if swallowed, and the wintergreen smell can make it seem like something to drink.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women are told by the label to check with a health professional first.

And three things not to do: do not bandage tightly over it, do not put a heat pack or heating pad on top, and do not use it on the face. Stop and see a doctor if the skin turns red and irritated, or if the pain lasts more than seven days.

Namman Muay compared with a Thai medicated oil

People sometimes put Namman Muay in the same basket as Siang Pure or Pim Saen. They are different tools. Those are small bottles of concentrated oil you dab on the temples, under the nose or on a bite, and many people also sniff them. Namman Muay is a body rub for muscles, used by the palmful, and it is not meant to be inhaled or put near the nose. Our guide to Thai and Chinese medicated oils covers the small-bottle kind.

Questions people ask

What is Namman Muay used for?

The maker labels it for the temporary relief of minor aches and pains of muscles and joints, such as backache, strains, sprains, bruises and arthritis. In Thai boxing it is also rubbed in before training as part of the warm-up massage.

What are the ingredients in Namman Muay?

The active ingredients are methyl salicylate (31%) and menthol (1.25%), as filed by the maker for its US label. The base contains alcohol, mineral oil, eucalyptus oil, purified water and an emulsifier.

Is Namman Muay hot or cold?

It feels warming. Methyl salicylate is the main active and produces the heat; the small amount of menthol adds a cool tingle on top.

Can I use Namman Muay every day?

The label allows up to four applications a day for people aged 12 and over. Stop and see a doctor if the skin becomes irritated or the pain lasts more than seven days.

Can I use Namman Muay if I am allergic to aspirin?

Ask a doctor first. Methyl salicylate belongs to the same salicylate family as aspirin, and the maker's label carries that warning.
