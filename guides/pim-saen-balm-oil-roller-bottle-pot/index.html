Pim Saen balm oil: bottle, pot or roll-on?

One Poy-Sian formula of menthol, camphor and borneol in three formats. How each is used, how it travels, and which one suits you.

By Dan Ler, Siam Supply Co.  ·  Published 3 November 2026

Pim Saen balm oil is one Thai formula sold in three formats: a small bottle, a pot and a roll-on. It is made under the Poy-Sian brand, and its main ingredients are menthol, camphor and borneol. You can sniff it, dab it under the nose or on the temples, or massage it in for a blocked nose, dizziness and headache. The formats differ in how you carry and use it, not in what is inside.

The name tells you what it is. Pim saen (พิมเสน) is the Thai word for borneol, the cooling crystal used in Thai remedies, and the full Thai name on the label, pim saen nam, means liquid borneol. It is the sister product of the Poy-Sian Mark II inhaler, from the same Bangkok maker, Gold Mints Products. This guide sets the three formats side by side so you can pick the one that suits you, or your shelf.

What is in Pim Saen

The maker lists menthol, camphor and borneol as the main ingredients. It is a clear, thin oil with a sharp, clean smell, closer to a Thai inhaler than to a balm. That is no accident: the same three crystals are the backbone of the Poy-Sian Mark II, whose label lists menthol 42.0%, camphor 16.4% and borneol 6.1% with eucalyptus oil. If you want the background on those ingredients, our guide to what yadom is covers them.

Because it is a liquid, Pim Saen does two jobs. You can breathe it in like an inhaler, or put a little on the skin like a medicated oil. Most of its fans use it both ways.

The bottle

The traditional format: a little glass bottle of liquid with a green cap. You tip a drop onto a fingertip and rub it on the temples, or hold the open bottle under your nose. It is the format older users grew up with, and the cheapest way to buy it.

The pot

A short, wide pot with a screw lid and a tear seal. The oil is soaked into cotton inside, so there is no free liquid to spill. You unscrew it and breathe from it like a herb jar, or touch a fingertip to the cotton to put a little on the skin. It is the most popular format for keeping on a desk or a car dashboard.

The roll-on

A slim glass bottle with a roller ball under a gold cap. You roll the oil straight onto the skin, so there is no dripping and no oily fingers. If you mainly use Pim Saen on the go, on the temples during a headache or under the nose on the MRT, this is the one.

How to use it

  • To clear a blocked nose: open the bottle or pot and breathe in slowly, one nostril at a time. Keep it a little way from the nose; there is no need to touch it.
  • For dizziness or travel sickness: a slow sniff, or a little rolled under the nose.
  • For a headache: a small amount on each temple, massaged in, well away from the eyes.

Cap it tightly after use. The oil is volatile, and an open bottle or pot loses strength fast.

Who should not use it

Pim Saen is strong. Keep it away from the eyes and the inside of the nose, do not use it on broken skin, and wash your hands after touching it. Camphor and menthol products are not for children under two; for a baby, an onion-based sticker such as Happy Noz is the gentler Thai option. If you have asthma or react to strong scents, ask a pharmacist first.

Pim Saen and the Poy-Sian inhaler

If you already use the Poy-Sian Mark II, Pim Saen will feel familiar. The inhaler is a dry stick with a small roll-on at the other end; Pim Saen is the liquid on its own, in a larger amount. People who use the inhaler all day often keep a Pim Saen pot at home or in the car as the refill-sized version. Our comparison of six Thai inhaler brands sets the Mark II against the others.

Buying Pim Saen in Singapore

You will find it at Thai supermarkets, provision shops and online, sometimes spelt Pim Sean or Pimsaen. Check the label for the Poy-Sian name and the green and red design. Questions people ask

What is Pim Saen balm oil used for?

For a blocked nose, dizziness, travel sickness and headache. You can sniff it from the bottle or pot, or put a little on the temples or under the nose.

What does Pim Saen mean?

Pim saen (พิมเสน) is the Thai word for borneol. The full name on the label, pim saen nam, means liquid borneol.

What are the ingredients in Pim Saen?

The maker lists menthol, camphor and borneol as the main ingredients.

Is the Pim Saen pot the same as the bottle?

It is the same oil. In the pot it is held in cotton, so it does not spill and you sniff it like a jar; the bottle holds it as free liquid.

Who makes Pim Saen?

It is a Poy-Sian product, made in Bangkok by Gold Mints Products Co., Ltd., the maker of the Poy-Sian Mark II inhaler.
