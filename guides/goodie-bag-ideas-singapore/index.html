For buyers

Goodie bag ideas that people actually keep: a Singapore buyer's guide

What goes into a goodie bag that gets used rather than binned, sized and priced for community clubs, corporate packs, marathons and school events.

By Dan Ler, Siam Supply Co.  ·  Published 23 October 2026

The best goodie bag ideas are small, useful and already familiar. The things that survive the trip home are the ones people use within a week: a pocket inhaler, a balm stick, a small repellent spray, a roll-on oil. The things that get binned are novelty items, heavy paper and anything that needs explaining.

Goodie bags are where our largest repeat orders come from. Community clubs, residents' networks and welfare groups packing hundreds of bags at a time, and HR teams doing staff wellness packs. After enough of these orders a pattern is obvious: the organisers who come back are the ones whose recipients actually used what was in the bag. This guide is what we have learned about choosing those items.

What makes an item worth putting in

  • It gets used within a week. A useful item gets remembered. A keychain goes in a drawer.
  • It is small and flat. Bags are packed by volunteers and carried home on the MRT. Anything bulky or fragile slows packing and gets left behind.
  • People already know it. A Poy-Sian inhaler needs no explaining to an auntie at a void-deck event. Nobody has to read a leaflet.
  • It suits everyone in the room. Unisex, no sizes, no allergens in food, nothing that only half the recipients can use.
  • It keeps. No short-dated food. Sealed remedies last well past the event.

Picks by who receives the bag

Community clubs and residents' events

Older residents are the core audience, and they already use Thai remedies. Poy-Sian two-in-one inhalers and Siang Pure ball-tip oils are the reliable picks: familiar, used daily, and small enough for any bag. For the difference between the Siang Pure formulas, see Formula 1 vs Formula 2.

Corporate and staff wellness packs

Office recipients want something that looks modern on a desk. Peppermint Field inhalers are the lightest scent of the Thai range and the easiest to use in an office, and Pastel balm sticks in fruit scents read as a cosmetic rather than a remedy. Our Thai inhaler comparison explains how the brands differ.

Runs, walks and sports days

Early starts outdoors call for two things: something for muscles and something for mosquitoes. Namman Muay, the Thai boxing liniment, is rubbed in before exercise, and a small SOFFELL spray covers the mosquitoes at the start line.

Schools and family days

Mild, fruit-scented, and nothing in an open jar. Pastel balm sticks work well here. For very young children, skip menthol and camphor products altogether; they are not for children under two.

Festive and seasonal bags

For Chinese New Year, Hari Raya or Deepavali packs going to older recipients, a balm or oil is a traditional, practical gift. During haze season, the Happy Noz orange PM2.5 sticker is a thoughtful addition to a family bag; see our Happy Noz colour guide.

Mix the bag, not the budget

A good bag usually combines one "hero" item people will recognise with one or two smaller ones. An inhaler plus a balm stick, or an oil plus a repellent, covers more of the recipients than three of the same thing. Because we sell by line, not by bag, mixing costs nothing extra: each line has its own minimum, and they all ship in one delivery.

Before you order

  1. Count, then add spares. Take the final headcount and add a few percent for torn bags, late sign-ups and the committee.
  2. Check each line's minimum. In our range, Poy-Sian and Pastel X8 start at 120 pieces; SOFFELL and Pastel balm sticks at 144. Our lowest minimum is 60 pieces, on Takabb.
  3. Allow for lead time. About five working days for lines we hold in stock, and up to four weeks for anything brought in on the next shipment from Thailand. We tell you which applies when we quote.
  4. Put it in one order. Minimums are per line, not per order, so the whole bag arrives in a single delivery to your door.

Institutional buyers such as community clubs, schools and companies can ask for Net 30 terms; payment on delivery is the standard.

What to leave out

  • Short-dated food. It expires before the bags are packed, and dietary needs vary.
  • Glass and liquids that can leak in a bag, unless sealed. Roller bottles and pump sprays travel better than open-cap oils.
  • Novelty items with the event logo that nobody uses after the day.
  • Anything that needs a leaflet to explain it.

Questions people ask

What are good goodie bag items for adults in Singapore?

Small, useful items people already know: pocket inhalers such as Poy-Sian, roll-on oils such as Siang Pure, balm sticks, and small repellent sprays such as SOFFELL.

What should I put in a corporate goodie bag?

Modern-looking, desk-friendly items: Peppermint Field inhalers, which have the lightest scent, and fruit-scented Pastel balm sticks.

How far ahead should I order goodie bag items?

At least a week for lines in stock, and up to four weeks for lines brought in from Thailand on the next shipment.

Can I mix different items in one wholesale order?

Yes. With us, minimums apply per line, not per order, so any combination ships together in one delivery.
