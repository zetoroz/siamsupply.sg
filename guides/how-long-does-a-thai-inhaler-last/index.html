How long does a Thai inhaler last, and how to make it last longer

Why yadom fades, which formats keep their scent longest, how to tell when one is finished, and four habits that make it last.

By Dan Ler, Siam Supply Co.  ·  Published 10 November 2026

A Thai inhaler lasts as long as its scent does, and that depends mostly on how much material it holds and how often it is left open. A herb jar keeps going the longest, because it is packed with loose herbs and crystals. A cotton-wick pot comes next. A slim pocket stick fades soonest, since its small wick holds the least oil. Whatever the format, capping it straight after each sniff and keeping it out of the heat are what stretch its life.

There is no fixed number of days on the box, and anyone who gives you one is guessing, because use varies so much. One person takes two sniffs on the MRT; another keeps the jar open on a desk all afternoon. What we can tell you is why inhalers fade, which formats hold out, how to tell when one is done, and how to make yours last. If you are new to them, start with our guide to what yadom is.

Why an inhaler fades

The smell and the cold rush come from menthol, camphor, borneol and plant oils. All of them are volatile: they turn into vapour at room temperature, which is exactly how an inhaler works. The catch is that they keep evaporating whether or not you are breathing them in. Every minute the cap is off, some of the inhaler is drifting into the room instead of your nose.

Heat speeds that up. A stick left on a car dashboard in the Singapore sun loses more in an afternoon than one in a drawer loses in weeks.

Which formats last longest

  1. The herb jar lasts longest. A screw-top jar such as Hong Thai is full of loose herbs and crystals, far more material than any stick. Many users top it up with a few drops of their own oil when it weakens, which a sealed stick does not allow.
  2. The cotton-wick pot comes next. A pot such as Pim Saen holds a decent amount of liquid oil in cotton under a screw lid. Our guide to the three Pim Saen formats explains how the pot compares with the bottle and roll-on.
  3. The two-in-one is in the middle. The inhaler end of a Poy-Sian Mark II or Peppermint Field is a stick, but you also get the small roll-on at the other end, which outlasts the inhaler side for most people.
  4. The pocket stick fades soonest. A slim stick such as the Siang Pure inhaler or Pastel X8 holds the least oil. It is the most convenient to carry and the first to go quiet.

Our comparison of six Thai inhaler brands covers the feel and strength of each, which also affects how long it seems to last: a strong formula still feels useful when a mild one has already faded.

How to make an inhaler last longer

  • Cap it straight away. Sniff, then close it. Do not leave a jar open on the desk between uses.
  • Keep it out of the heat. Not on the dashboard, not in a bag in the sun, not beside a laptop vent. A pocket or a drawer is fine.
  • Screw the lid on fully. On jars and pots, a lid that is only half turned leaks vapour all day.
  • Keep your own. This is about hygiene as much as life: the tip touches your nose, so sharing an inhaler means sharing whatever is on it.

How to tell when it is finished

An inhaler does not go off in a dramatic way; it just fades. The signs are simple:

  • You need several deep breaths to feel what one used to give you.
  • The smell has gone flat or dusty rather than sharp and cold.
  • In a jar, the herbs look dry and pale and the crystals have disappeared.
  • The expiry date printed on the pack has passed. Most Thai inhalers carry one on the label or the cap.

Once it reaches that point, replace it. An old inhaler is not dangerous, it simply no longer does its job, and a stick that has been in and out of your nose for months is worth replacing for hygiene anyway.

Unopened stock

A sealed inhaler keeps far longer than an opened one, because the seal stops the evaporation. Store unopened stock somewhere cool and out of direct sun, use the oldest first, and go by the expiry date on the pack. If you run a shop, that means keeping inhalers off a sunny shop-front display and away from the top shelf under the lights.

Questions people ask

How long does a Thai inhaler last?

It depends on the format and how you use it. A herb jar lasts longest, a cotton-wick pot next, and a slim pocket stick fades soonest. Capping it after every sniff and keeping it out of the heat make the biggest difference.

How long does yadom last once opened?

There is no fixed time, because it depends on how often it is left open and how warm it is kept. When you need several deep breaths to feel it, or the smell has gone flat, it is time for a new one.

Can I refill a Thai inhaler?

Herb jars are often topped up with a few drops of oil when they weaken. Sealed pocket sticks are not designed to be refilled and are better replaced.

Do Thai inhalers expire?

Yes. Most carry an expiry date on the label or cap. An expired inhaler is usually just weaker, but go by the date on the pack.

Why does my inhaler stop working so fast?

Usually because it is left open or kept somewhere hot, such as a car. The active ingredients evaporate at room temperature, and heat speeds that up.
